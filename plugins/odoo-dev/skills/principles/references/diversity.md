# Rule of Diversity

**Statement:** Distrust all claims for "one true way."

## Rationale

No single paradigm, methodology, tool, or architecture is universally optimal. Different problems have different shapes; different contexts have different constraints; different scales expose different tradeoffs. A claim that one approach should be used everywhere is almost always the result of overgeneralizing from a limited set of cases where that approach happened to work well.

Monocultures are fragile. A system or ecosystem in which everyone uses the same tool, follows the same pattern, or makes the same tradeoff has a single point of conceptual failure: a flaw in that shared approach affects everything simultaneously.

Diversity does not mean that all approaches are equally valid for a given problem. It means the choice of approach should be driven by the specific requirements of the problem, not by allegiance to a methodology.

## Corollaries

- Evaluate tools and approaches against the specific requirements at hand, not against abstract claims of superiority.
- Best practices are practices that are best in the context where they were developed. Verify that the context matches before adopting the practice.
- The strongest designs borrow good ideas from multiple traditions rather than committing to one.
- When everyone on a team reaches for the same solution regardless of the problem, that is a warning sign.

## Guards Against

- Cargo-culting: adopting practices because they worked elsewhere without understanding why they worked.
- Framework lock-in that prevents adopting better approaches for specific problems.
- Architectural dogmatism that fits the system to the methodology rather than the methodology to the system.
